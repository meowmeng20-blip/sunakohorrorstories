HACHISHAKUSAMA (EIGHT FEET TALL)

Sender: Yumiko • Japanese Supernatural Legend

My grandparents lived in Japan. Every Summer, my parents would take me there on holiday to visit them. They lived in a small village and they had a big backyard. I loved to play there during the Summer. When we arrived, my grandparents always welcomed me with open arms. I was their only grandchild, so they spoiled me.

The last time I saw them was the Summer when I was 8 years old.

As usual, my parents booked a flight to Japan and we drove from the airport to my grandparents' house. They were delighted to see me and had a lot of little presents to give me. My parents wanted to have some time by themselves, so after a few days, they took a trip to another part of Japan, leaving me in the care of my Grandma and Grandpa.

One day, I was playing out in the backyard. My grandparents were inside the house. It was a hot Summer’s day and I lay down on the grass to rest. I stared up at the clouds and enjoyed the feeling of the soft rays of the sun and the gentle breeze. Just as I was about to get up, I heard a strange sound.

“Po… Po… Po… Po… Po… Po… Po…”

I didn’t know what it was and it was hard to figure out where it was coming from. It sounded almost like somebody was making the noise themselves… as if they were just saying, “Po… Po… Po…” over and over again in a deep, masculine voice.

I was looking around, searching for the source of the noise when I suddenly noticed something on top of the tall hedges that enclosed the backyard. It was a straw hat. It wasn’t resting on the hedge, it was behind it. That’s where the sound was coming from.

Then, the hat began to move, as if someone was wearing it. The hat stopped at a small gap in the hedge and I could see a face peering through. It was a woman. But, the hedges were high… almost 8 feet tall…

I was surprised at how tall the woman was. I wondered if she was wearing stilts or some sort of huge high-heeled shoes. Then, a split second later, she walked off and the strange noise disappeared with her, fading into the distance.

Bewildered, I got up and wandered back into the house. My grandparents were in the kitchen drinking tea. I sat down at the table and, after a while, I told my grandparents what I had seen. They weren’t really paying attention to me, until I mentioned that distinctive sound.

As soon as I said “Po… Po… Po…”, both of them suddenly froze. Grandma’s eyes grew wide and she covered her mouth with her hand. Grandpa’s face became very serious and he grabbed me by the arm.

“This is very important,” he said, in an intense voice. “You must tell us exactly… How tall was she?”

“As tall as the garden hedge,” I replied, beginning to get scared.

My grandfather bombarded me with questions: “Where was she standing? When did this happen? What did you do? Did she see you?”

I tried to answer all his questions as best I could. He suddenly rushed out to the hallway and made a phone call. I couldn’t hear what he was saying. I looked over at my Grandma and she was trembling.

Grandpa came barging back into the room and spoke to my grandmother. “I’ve got to go out for a while. You stay here with the child. Don’t take your eyes off him for a second.”

“What’s going on, Grandpa?” I cried.

He looked at me with a sad expression in his eyes and said, “You’ve been liked by Hachishakusama.”

With that, he hurried out, got into his truck and drove off.

What is Hachishakusama?

My grandmother explained that "Hachishakusama" means "Eight Feet Tall" in Japanese. Anyone who sees Eight Feet Tall is destined to die within a few days. She always chooses children as her victims because they are easily deceived when she poses as their relatives.

When Grandpa came back, there was an old woman with him named K-san. She handed me a small crumpled piece of parchment and said, “Here, take this and hold it.” They brought me into my bedroom. The windows were covered in newspaper with ancient runes written on them. Small bowls of salt were in all four corners, and a Buddha figure sat in the center.

K-san sat me down and instructed: “You must stay in this room until 7 o’clock tomorrow morning. You must not come out under any circumstances. Your grandparents will not speak to you until then. Never let go of the parchment. If anything happens, pray to Buddha.”

I locked the door and waited. Around 1:00 AM, I woke up to tapping on the window.

Tap, Tap, Tap, Tap, Tap…

Then I heard Grandpa’s voice outside the door: “Are you OK in there? If you’re scared, I can come in and keep you company.”

I rushed to open the door, but stopped. The voice sounded like Grandpa, but something was terribly wrong. I glanced to my left and gasped—the salt in all four bowls was slowly turning pitch black.

“Po… Po… Po… Po… Po… Po… Po…”

I fell to my knees in front of the Buddha statue, clutching the parchment, half-crying and praying all night. By morning, the salt was pitch black, but I had survived the night.

At 7:30 AM, K-san, my grandparents, my parents, and eight blood-relative men from the village put me in a 9-seater van to evacuate me out of the village. As we drove, Hachishakusama kept pace alongside the van, tapping frantically on the windows and echoing her terrifying voice.

Thanks to K-san's prayers and the protective convoy, we made it to the airport. I was forced to leave Japan forever. Anyone liked by her who survives must settle in foreign countries and can never set foot in their homeland again.

That happened more than 10 years ago. My grandfather passed away two years ago, strictly forbidding me from attending his funeral in his will to keep me safe.

A few days ago, my grandmother called me on the phone. She said she had been diagnosed with cancer and wanted to see me one last time before she dies.

“Are you sure, Grandma?” I asked. “Is it safe?”

“It’s been 10 years,” she said. “It’s all forgotten. You’re grown up now. I’m sure there won’t be a problem.”

“But… what about Hachishakusama?” I asked.

For a moment, there was complete silence on the other end of the line. Then, I heard a deep, masculine voice speaking through the receiver...

“Po… Po… Po… Po… Po… Po… Po…”
