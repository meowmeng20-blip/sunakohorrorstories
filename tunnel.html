THE JAPANESE TUNNEL BENEATH SOUTH KOREA

Sender: Han Ji-hoon • Underground Facility

Classified Incident:

Construction workers expanding an underground railway line discovered a sealed tunnel. It wasn't on any map or government record. According to historians... it should not have existed at all.

My name is Han Ji-hoon, and I worked as a safety inspector during a railway expansion project outside Busan. The project had been running smoothly for months. Everything changed on a rainy afternoon when a drilling machine broke through what appeared to be an artificial passage hidden behind solid stone. The opening revealed a narrow tunnel reinforced with aging concrete and rusted steel supports. Initial surveys suggested the structure was decades old. Historians called to inspect the site became visibly uncomfortable—several believed the tunnel dated back to the Japanese occupation period.

The discovery attracted immediate attention. Engineers entered first, followed by survey teams. The tunnel stretched far deeper than expected. Every few hundred feet, strange symbols appeared painted along the walls—some Japanese, others completely unfamiliar. The deeper investigators traveled, the colder the air became. Electronic equipment began malfunctioning, batteries drained unexpectedly, and radio communications suffered repeated interference.

Three days after the discovery, a larger exploration team entered. Survey equipment detected a massive chamber further underground. Massive concrete pillars surrounded a circular structure in the center of the chamber. It didn't resemble a military bunker; it looked deliberately constructed around something.

Terrifying Discovery:

"One technician noticed scratch marks covering the concrete walls. Thousands of them. Deep grooves carved into the surface as if something had repeatedly tried to escape."

Multiple cameras recorded brief shadows moving beyond flashlight range simultaneously from different angles. Team members reported hearing distant footsteps, whispers in Japanese, and one worker insisted he heard someone calling his name from deeper inside the darkness.

That evening, three members of the team refused to discuss what they witnessed and requested immediate reassignment. Unofficially, rumors spread that the men had seen someone standing inside the chamber—someone who could not possibly have been there.

Government officials soon secured the site. Historians reviewing archives uncovered fragmented references to a classified Japanese military construction project during the final years of occupation: an underground containment facility.

Months passed before authorities announced the site would be permanently sealed with tons of reinforced concrete, citing structural instability. Publicly, the matter was closed.

Years later, an earthquake struck the region, damaging portions of the sealed site. Inspectors sent to evaluate discovered something alarming: the concrete barrier remained intact, yet fresh footprints appeared beyond it, leading deeper into the tunnel.

Final Footage Transmission:

Last year, a maintenance drone entered the tunnel for the first time in nearly a decade. It transmitted video successfully for seventeen minutes until it reached the central chamber.

The final frame showed a human-shaped figure standing beside the circular structure. The figure turned toward the camera... and the transmission ended. The drone was never recovered.
