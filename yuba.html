THE YUBA COUNTY FIVE

Sender: Jenny • Nightly Reads

In February 1978, five young men from California, all of whom had mild intellectual disabilities or psychiatric conditions, went to watch a college basketball game. They were close friends who all lived with their parents.

After the game, they stopped at a convenience store for snacks and then started the drive home. But they never made it.

A few days later, their car was found abandoned on a remote, snow-covered mountain dirt road way past the snow line, far in the opposite direction of their homes. There was absolutely nothing wrong with the car. It had plenty of gas, it wasn't stuck in the mud or snow, and the engine worked perfectly. For some reason, instead of driving back down the mountain or staying inside the warm vehicle, the five men got out wearing only light clothing and walked into the freezing wilderness in the middle of the night.

Months later, when the snow finally melted, a gruesome discovery was made. The remains of four of the men were found in and around a forest service trailer about 19 miles away from where they had left the car.

The Chilling Scene:

"One of the men, Ted Weiher, was found tucked into a bed inside the trailer. Based on his beard growth and weight loss, examiners determined he had survived in that bed for an estimated 8 to 13 weeks before finally starving to death. He had lost nearly half his body weight and suffered severe frostbite."

But the trailer was fully stocked. There was a propane tank outside to heat the cabin, and matches were laying on a table. None of it was turned on or used. There were heavy winter coats and blankets scattered around the cabin that no one put on. Even more disturbing, there was an unlocked storage shed attached to the trailer packed with enough freeze-dried food to keep all five of them alive for a year. Only a few cans had been opened. A manual can opener was sitting in the cabin, but whoever opened the cans had used a rock or a pocket knife to pry them open instead.

It was as if they were too terrified to leave the bed, or their cognitive disabilities prevented them from understanding that they were surrounded by everything they needed to survive.

Unsolved Mystery:

"The fifth man, Gary Mathias—who was the only one with a history of severe schizophrenia and who actually had military survival training—was never found. They only found his shoes left behind in the trailer."

Nobody knows what chased them up that mountain, why they abandoned a perfectly working car, or why they just laid in the freezing dark slowly starving to death in a cabin full of food.

Some mysteries remain frozen in time, leaving us to wonder what terror truly awaited them on that mountain.
