CRIMSON SMILE: THE TALE OF THE SLIT-MOUTHED WOMAN

Sender: Samson • Urban Legends

Chapter 1:

The Curfew

It started as a rumor in the neighborhood, a whispered warning that everyone in the quiet town of Sakuragi-cho laughed off. It was just a silly story, parents telling children to stay indoors after sunset. A cautionary tale to keep kids out of trouble. “Don’t walk alone at night, or Kuchisake-onna will get you.” We used to joke about it while playing tag in the playground.

But I should have known better than to dismiss it. After all, urban legends don’t appear out of thin air.

I was 17-year-old Akio, and I was on my way home from a late-night study session. The streetlights flickered, casting eerie, dancing shadows on the deserted sidewalk. The air was crisp, typical for an autumn night, but tonight, it felt colder, heavier. The silence was unsettling, only broken by the occasional distant bark of a dog and the rhythmic tap, tap, tap of my own shoes against the pavement.

My headphones were blasted with music, a desperate attempt to drown out the creeping anxiety in my gut. I checked my phone: 11:45 PM. Shit. I was past my curfew. My mom would kill me.

The path home led me through a narrow, dimly lit alleyway flanked by towering, aged buildings. The graffiti on the walls looked like grotesque, contorted faces in the weak moonlight. A sudden, sharp shiver ran down my spine, unrelated to the cold. It was the primal instinct of a cornered animal realizing it’s no longer at the top of the food chain.

I stopped in my tracks. The sound of my own breath felt amplified.

There, at the other end of the alley, a figure stood, bathed in the faint glow of the furthest streetlight. A woman.

She seemed ordinary at first. Slender, dressed in a long, beige trench coat that reached her shins, and her head was tilted slightly downwards, obscure her features. A thick, knitted scarf was wrapped snugly around her lower face. Probably protecting herself from the cold, I thought, my heart rate slowing down a little.

But as I cautiously approached, something felt wrong. The atmosphere shifted, becoming suffocatingly still. Even the distant city sounds vanished, leaving only a deafening, unnatural silence. The only noise was the thump, thump of my heart, pounding like a trapped bird.

She wasn’t moving. She just stood there, watching me.

I tried to walk around her, sticking close to the opposite wall. As I got closer, I noticed details that escaped me before. She was tall, unusually tall. And her trench coat... it was stained in dark, suspicious-looking patches.

"Ex...excuse me," I stammered, my voice barely a whisper. I tried to push past her, but she stepped into my path, a smooth, graceful, predatory motion.

Chapter 2:

The Encounter

She didn't speak immediately. She just gazed at me with vacant, soulless eyes that seemed to pull me into a vortex of despair. The air grew frigid, and my breath fogged in front of me. I felt like a lamb that had willingly walked into the wolf’s den.

Suddenly, a voice, raspy and dry, like dead leaves rustling, pierced the silence.

"Watashi, kirei?" (Am I beautiful?)

My blood ran cold. The urban legend crashed down on me like a tidal wave. This wasn't just a story. This was real. This was happening to me.

I swallowed the lump of fear in my throat, trying to recall the survival rules from the playground games. What were they again? If you say 'No', she kills you. If you say 'Yes', she... wait, what was the other one?

"Y-yes," I replied, my voice cracking. "You are... very beautiful."

A tense moment of silence. My answer seemed to appease her, if only for a second. Her eyes flickered, perhaps with a brief moment of sadistic gratification. Then, slowly, terrifyingly, she began to unravel the knitted scarf from her face.

I couldn't look away. I was paralyzed by a sick curiosity, a morbid fascination with my impending doom. The fabric slid off, revealing her nose, her pale lips, and then...

A guttural sob escaped my throat.

Her mouth was a grotesque, bloody scar, surgically slit from one ear to the other, creating a terrifying, permanent crimson grin. The flesh was raw, revealing rows of teeth that looked sharpened into wicked points. A trickle of blood oozed from one corner of her mouth, painting a stark contrast against her unnaturally white skin.

"Kore demo?" (Even like this?)

She was smiling—or rather, a caricature of a smile, a frozenmask of pure, unadulterated madness. I stared into that hideous orifice, seeing the abyss yawning before me. The horror was suffocating, crushing the air out of my lungs.

Chapter 3:

The Game

My instinct for self-preservation finally kicked in, overriding my paralysis. I screamed, a raw, primal sound of terror that echoed through the empty alleyway. I turned and bolted in the opposite direction, my legs pumping furiously. I didn't care about my phone, my backpack, or the curfew anymore. I only wanted to escape.

But she was faster. I heard the clack, clack, clack of her heels close behind me, growing louder, faster. I didn't dare look back. I knew she was right there.

"You said yes," her voice rang out, a chilling sound that sent a fresh wave of panic through me. "We have to match."

She lunged, her long trench coat flapping in the wind. A glint of metal caught my eye—a pair of large, industrial-looking surgical scissors, shining menacingly in the faint light. She was close, close enough to smell the faint scent of antiseptics and old, dried blood.

I tripped over a loose brick and tumbled to the ground, scraping my knees. Pain seared through my legs, but it was overshadowed by the looming terror. I scrambled backwards on my hands and knees, my back pressing against the cold, rough wall of a building. It was a dead end.

I was cornered.

Kuchisake-onna loomed over me, the surgical scissors raised high. She wasn't human. Her eyes were blank pools of darkness, her monstrous crimson grin wider than ever.

"A-average!" I yelled, recalling the forgotten third rule from the legends. I tried to sound confident, but my voice was a trembling mess. "You are... average looking!"

She hesitated. The hand holding the scissors froze mid-air. Her head tilted, as if processing my statement. A look of confusion flickered in her empty eyes.

That was my chance. I scrambled to my feet, using every ounce of adrenaline to push past her. I didn't even look back this time. I ran, faster than I’ve ever run in my entire life, until my lungs burned and my legs felt like they would give out.

Chapter 4:

The Reminder

I didn't stop running until I reached my house. I burst through the front door, slamming it behind me and locking it with trembling hands. My mom rushed into the hallway, alarm written across her face.

"Akio? What happened? Why are you covered in dirt?" she asked, looking from my scratched knees to my pale, terrified face.

I collapsed to the floor, my breath coming in ragged gasps. I couldn't find the words. I just pointed in the direction I had come from, shivering violently.

"I-I... I saw her," I managed to choke out.

"Saw who?"

I took a deep breath, trying to steady my voice. "The woman with the surgical mask. The Slit-Mouthed Woman."

My mom paled. She didn’t scold me for being late or make fun of my story. Instead, she helped me up, her face grim.

"You're home now, Akio," she whispered, her voice laced with an unusual seriousness. "Don't go out after dark anymore. Never again."

I spent the next few days in a state of constant fear. The smallest noise made me jump. Every woman I saw on the street, even during the day, made me suspicious. The image of that horrific, crimson smile was seared into my brain, haunting my dreams.

A few days later, a news report appeared in the local paper. A high school student had been found brutally attacked in a nearby neighborhood. His face had been horrifically slashed, the cuts perfectly mirroring the hideous grin of Kuchisake-onna. The investigation was ongoing, but the town was in a panic. The urban legend was no longer just a silly story. It was a terrifying reality.

I was lucky that night. I escaped with just a few scratches and a lifetime of psychological trauma. But every time I walk home now, even in broad daylight, I look over my shoulder. And when the sun starts to set and the sky turns a fiery red, a chill runs down my spine.

Because I know she's still out there. And she’s still looking for someone to match her crimson smile.
